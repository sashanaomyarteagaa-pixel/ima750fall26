Text Game Fuck My birthday’s coming up

Description: For the past 24 years, I have constantly had this internal battle with myself: Should I celebrate my birthday this year and cry about it later? Or should I just cry about it till it's over? Every year my birthday comes, and I am a mess, not because it's too much work- well, it's that- but also because I have been depressed since the age of 5, and the thought of celebrating another year feels exhausting and frankly not appealing. January 25 is approaching, and I am trying to have a positive outlook on it for my sake. I have also been avoiding the idea of celebrating past birthdays that for some reason make this year a little different; perhaps it is because my frontal lobe is slowly arriving. In the age of AI, unemployment, moving back home, war, and this administration, the idea and celebration can sound unnecessary. As a 24-year-old and as most 24- to 25-year-olds, we have not exactly had the most promising future, but despite everything, why can’t celebrating just be about you and the accomplishment regardless of the things happening around us? This is the one day where you give thanks to the places your body has taken you, the things your hands have made, and the ideas that you have contributed to spaces. For my Imagine Otherwise project, my game proposes the question: Perhaps you are struggling to celebrate your birthday this year; what if you choose to celebrate, or what if you choose not to? There would be two initial options at the start of the game: What if I celebrate my birthday? And two: What if I don’t celebrate this year? Afterwards, depending on what the player chooses, they will go through a series of scenarios, scenarios that I have made up in my head for every year before contemplating doing something for my birthday. This game puts the player in the shoes of someone with anxiety around this event that other people seem to feel is a day of fun and an excuse to make the whole week about them and their existence. The goal of the game is to have the player choose scenarios that would hopefully make their character choose otherwise; in this case, the otherwise being to celebrate and enjoy it. The overall hope for this game is to make someone who is playing it understand the complexities of these simple options of whether to, or whether not to. It also encourages other people with the same dilemmas to go on a journey and unpack the reasons for not choosing otherwise. Or if they do choose otherwise, perhaps it will motivate them to anticipate a good outcome. (all of this is subject to change)
