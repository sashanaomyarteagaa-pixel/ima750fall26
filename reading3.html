Reading 3

Theory: As We May Think (scan); As We May Think (web) (by Vannevar Bush, 1945) & Inspiration: Man with a Movie Camera, 1929

“As We May Think” by Vannevar Bush gives us an insight into the perspective of an engineer and scientist at the end of World War II and his thoughts about the future of scientific technology. He notes the importance of information records to science and his predictions about how film would be a source of storage for factual information. This makes me think about documentary and how it is used today as a source of impact and a contributor to world changes on any scale. When watching the 1929 film “Man with a Movie Camera,” it shows that filmmakers were making scientific advances already way before the end of the Second World War, using documentation as a source for stored information, revealing how people interacted with each other, traditional celebrations, factory conditions and processes, and athletic training, which could connect with research on the human body and movement.

Bush may be one of the first people with predictions like this, but he was certainly not one of the first people to already be actively participating in new scientific technology. It shows how artists, although not actively creating scientific statements, have been actively making scientific history. Bush also predicted the creation of the internet. “The Memex” is one of his ideas that is not far off from our day-to-day internet use. It shows how fast technological advances have developed, especially now, how quickly AI has become stronger and smarter. Bush’s statement that the way they were stored back then “felt outdated” makes me wonder if the world before accessible AI was also considered outdated in how we managed information, or if this is a technological advancement that got too out of hand?
