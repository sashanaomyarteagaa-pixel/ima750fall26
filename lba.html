Lead In Our Bodies

Abstract: Through a series of archival family photographs, new photographs, and an original poem capturing life in the NYCHA projects in East Harlem, this visual essay examines lead (Heavy, post-transition metal) as a secret, unwanted member living under our roof for years. This project aims to raise awareness of the inconsistent NYCHA housing services that my family and many others should have received for years.

Project Description: The project “Lead in Our Bodies” aims to understand the severity of overlooked, dangerous living conditions and their impact on BIPOC residents in New York City. For context on why this project matters to me, I moved into the project at age 5 with my mother when we immigrated from Ecuador in 2007. Years later, I would have two baby brothers living under the same roof. We became a family of five in a two-bedroom, one-small-bathroom, lead-infused project building. Growing up, I do not remember how many times exactly my brothers and I got sick, but I do remember multiple home remedies my mom would whip up. We were sick often. After reading about the effects of what Lead can do to a developing child, which are: damage to the brain and nervous system, slowed growth and development, learning and behavior problems, hearing and speech problems, and that this can cause lower IQ, decreased ability to pay attention, and underperformance in school, I have found out that between my brothers and me, we checked off 4 on the list. They currently still live in the house at ages 14 & 18. Lead still on the walls. It raises questions: had action been taken sooner, would my brothers and I have grown up differently developmentally? Would that show up in our adulthood positively? Would mental illness in our family be something that could have been helped more easily without lead as a contributor? Especially now, knowing the causes of lead poisoning in adults, which are: High blood pressure, Joint and muscle pain, difficulties with memory or concentration, Headache, Abdominal pain, Mood disorders, reduced sperm count, and abnormal sperm, miscarriages, stillbirth, or premature birth. It questions if the relationship with my mother would have been healthier. Would the relationship between my stepdad and my mother have been less negative, less abusive? If we weren't living with lead, would my stepdad’s diabetes worsen over time? Now that I am an adult, is the reason for my constant body and joint aches due to the years of lead exposure? Is lead paint use in NYCHA an intentional project? Systemically, what is the real reason why we call it “the projects” in the first place? These are not just questions that I hope to answer one day, but are questions that could impact other people to think about their environment growing up due to the City’s and NYCHA’’S neglect. I hope it evokes anger and motivation to continue speaking up for ourselves and our neighbors. This project is a representation of one out of thousands of families underrepresented and forgotten; even though this project focuses on my family’s experience, it raises concerns for the 511,000 residents that NYCHA claims they “Serve”.
