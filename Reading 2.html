The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction (by Walter Benjamin, 1935) & AI’s AURA: Aesthetic, Ubiquitous, Regimented Automation (by Kalindi Vora and Neda Atanasoski, 2026)

Walter Benjamin claims, “It is significant that the existence of the work of art with references to its aura is never entirely separated from its ritual function. In other words, the unique value of the ‘authentic’ work of art has its basis in ritual, the location of its original use value…” When works of art are mechanically reproduced, they lose their aura, authenticity, and significance. There is something ritualistic about gathering in a space where the artwork took place or was born, spectating its original form. Even if the artwork is not religious in nature, many artworks become, as Benjamin calls them, “the secular cult of Beauty”; they have cult value. Take the Statue of Liberty as an example: for many who saw the Statue of Liberty in the days when many immigrants were arriving by boat, this piece of art symbolized freedom and new hope. For years, it has been reproduced into mini statues, prints, and keychains around the world. The reproduction of an artwork that was given as a gift meant for the City of New York loses its aura when reproduced for capitalist purposes; there is no cult value in holding a Statue of Liberty keychain in your pocket. Benjamin’s talk about art as ritual connects to how social protest performance also works in a similar way. When we talk about Luis Valdez with his theater company “El Teatro Campesino" and Amiri Baraka’s “Black Revolutionary Theatre,” their performances relied heavily on audience interaction in almost a ritualistic way, where they had active engagement in their art space that transferred to the outside world.

Now we enter a new world of AI, where art is constantly being reproduced, stolen, and generated. It makes me question how aura in art will soon disappear, and how fast humans will stop participating in rituals, considering it is in our nature from the beginning of time to do so. Even with a robot like the new Tesla Optimus, which is set to be released in 2027. This robot will be in charge of handling household chores such as washing dishes, so humans can be available to focus on things that are more important and in need of their intelligence (which is also being taken by AI).
